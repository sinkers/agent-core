# NEC4, FIDIC, equipment supply, EPC and long-term service agreements

Synthesis from public commentary (NEC, Fenwick Elliott, Norton Rose Fulbright, MinterEllison, Hamilton Locke, FBM,
HWL Ebsworth). Read the executed contract before relying on any of it.

## NEC4 Engineering and Construction Contract (ECC)

- Main options: A priced with activity schedule; B priced with bill of quantities; C target with activity schedule;
  D target with bill; E cost reimbursable; F management contract. Secondary options are the X clauses (e.g. X7 delay
  damages, X16 retention).
- **Early warning (cl 15)**: either side notifies as soon as it becomes aware of a matter that could increase cost,
  delay completion or impair performance; early warning register and meetings. If the contractor failed to give an
  early warning it should have, the compensation event is assessed as if it had (cl 61.5); under C-F, the extra cost
  can be Disallowed Cost.
- **Compensation events (cl 60-65)**: the contractor notifies within **8 weeks** of becoming aware (cl 61.3) or loses
  the right to a change in Prices and Completion Date (except events arising from the PM's own instructions).
  Quotations follow; the PM replies within set periods or the quotation may be deemed accepted. Track every period.
- **Programme (cl 31-32)**: the Accepted Programme is the baseline for assessing compensation events. The PM accepts
  or gives reasons for not accepting (cl 31.3). No accepted programme can mean the PM assesses events alone — keep
  the programme accepted and current.
- Fits collaborative, programme-driven projects; requires disciplined administration by both sides.

## FIDIC 2017 (second edition, reprinted 2022 with amendments)

- **Red Book**: employer designs; the Engineer administers. **Yellow Book**: contractor designs (plant and design-
  build); the Engineer administers. **Silver Book**: EPC/turnkey, contractor takes most risk, **no Engineer** (the
  Employer's Representative). Silver suits a lump-sum turnkey BESS or substation EPC; many OEMs offer their own
  Yellow-like supply terms.
- **Claims (cl 20.2)**: notice of claim within **28 days** of becoming aware, or the claim is barred (applies to
  Employer claims too); the Engineer may notify within 14 days that a notice is late, otherwise it's deemed valid.
  Fully detailed claim within **84 days**. The Engineer agrees or determines (cl 3.7); a notice of dissatisfaction
  within 28 days sends it to the **DAAB** (dispute avoidance/adjudication board, cl 21), then arbitration.

## Equipment supply and EPC for BESS and HV plant

- Australian BESS projects mostly use **split contracting**: a BESS supply (or supply-and-commission) contract with
  the OEM plus a balance-of-plant (civil, electrical, connection) contract, sometimes with an integrator. A full-wrap
  EPC is preferred by financiers but is scarce and costly.
- With split contracts the **owner carries the interface risk**. A division-of-responsibility (interface) matrix is
  essential: who supplies, installs, tests, commissions and warrants each item; who carries delivery delay; how
  OEM installation instructions bind the BoP contractor; warranty gaps.
- Owner-supplied equipment in a construction contract: define delivery point and date, risk and title transfer,
  inspection on receipt, storage, who installs to the OEM's instructions, and whose delay it is if it's late.
- Standard Australian form: AS 4910-2002 (supply of equipment with installation).
- Check: performance guarantees and tests (capacity, round-trip efficiency, availability), performance LDs and caps,
  delay LDs on supply, FAT/SAT procedures and witness rights, spares, warranties and their conditions, the measurement
  point (DC terminals or point of connection — use the same one in supply and service contracts), shipping terms
  (Incoterms), import duties, FX.

## Long-term service agreements (LTSAs)

Typically 5-20 years: preventive and corrective maintenance, spares, availability guarantees, capacity/state-of-
health and round-trip efficiency guarantees tied to a reference performance test, augmentation against the agreed
duty cycle, and warranty conditions (cycling, state of charge, temperature). Align the LTSA with the supply
warranty and the operating strategy (trading cycles count against warranty limits).

## Early contractor involvement (ECI)

Two stages: stage 1 (paid) develops design, programme, risks and price, often with long-lead procurement; stage 2 is
the D&C contract if the price is accepted. Benefits: buildability, early ordering, price certainty. Risks: loss of
competitive tension, the contractor not proceeding, stage 1 costs not recovered. The ECI agreement must say what
happens if stage 2 isn't agreed (IP, design, payment).

## Letters of intent and reservation agreements

To hold a transformer factory slot or start early works: cap the authorised spend, define the scope, state whether
a contract exists, the payment basis, termination for convenience, title to anything made, and an end date. Lawyer
review every time.
